# Energy information needs of SMB owners and facility managers with on-site solar, storage, and EV charging

Research date: 2026-11-08. Scope: US/California small-to-mid-sized businesses (SMBs). Other geographies are labeled. Vendor material is labeled **[vendor]**. Old evidence is flagged by year. The search budget was capped at about 18 tool calls, so the Gaps sections matter: several requested vendor products (Enphase commercial, Generac PWRfleet, Span commercial, Wattbuild, Schneider) could not be documented from first-party sources.

## Which metrics do commercial solar+storage owners prioritize, and which drive decisions?

### Takeaway
I found no survey, with a stated sample size, of US commercial solar+storage *owners* about which dashboard metrics they prioritize. That is a real gap in the public record. The indirect evidence points one way. Cost reduction ranks well above carbon for businesses generally. Demand-charge reduction is the main economic reason for commercial storage. Solar alone does little for demand charges, so a dashboard serving commercial owners has to attribute savings to demand ($/kW) and energy ($/kWh) separately.

### Cited Findings
- **Small businesses care most about cost (US, fielded Nov 2025).** In an NFIB Small Business Energy Survey, about 80% of owners said energy costs significantly affect their operations, and many said they lack viable ways to reduce energy expenses. The full sample size was not retrieved because the press page returned 403. — [NFIB press release](https://www.nfib.com/news/press-release/new-nfib-report-how-energy-costs-impact-small-businesses-2/); [NFIB survey release](https://www.nfib.com/news/press-release/nfib-releases-new-small-business-energy-survey/)
- **Cost ranks above carbon (global, older, about 2021–2022, check date).** In an ABB Energy Insights survey of about 2,300 business leaders, 61% named reducing energy costs as their top priority, while only 40% included carbon reduction among business priorities. 49% said they lack the know-how to act on energy challenges. — [ABB press release](https://new.abb.com/news/detail/100874/research-reveals-energy-concerns-impacting-business-competitiveness-the-workforce-and-decarbonization)
- **Small firms save less and manage energy less formally (global, 2025).** The IEA Industrial Competitiveness Survey covered 1,000 companies in 14 countries. Firms under 100 employees reported about 12% average savings from efficiency actions, versus nearly 20% for larger firms. The IEA attributes the gap to limited financial and human resources and less formal energy management. — [IEA commentary](https://www.iea.org/commentaries/businesses-see-competitive-value-of-energy-efficiency-but-smaller-firms-struggle-to-access-solutions)
- **UK small businesses (Jan 2025, n=255, UK only).** 88% said they prioritize some aspects of sustainability more than a year earlier. Energy consumption and renewables reliance led the list. — [Leasing Life on the Novuna survey](https://www.leasinglife.com/news/small-businesses-prioritise-sustainability-2025/)
- **Demand-charge savings by technology (LBNL/NREL, 2017, modeled, not surveyed).** Across 15 commercial building types in 15 US locations, median demand-charge reduction was about 42% for solar+storage, versus about 8% for solar alone (a secondary summary says 7%) and about 23% for storage alone. Combined savings almost always exceeded the sum of the separate savings. — [LBNL publication page](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/publications/solar-storage-synergies-managing); [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1408479)
- **Solar alone saves little on non-coincident demand charges (LBNL, Darghouth, Barbose et al., 2017).** Under a basic non-coincident demand charge, most commercial customers save little because their load profiles don't line up with PV output. Some customers still reach 20% or more. Savings are higher when the tariff uses predefined daytime peak periods or longer averaging intervals. — [LBNL "Exploring Demand Charge Savings from Commercial Solar"](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/bibcite/reference/29690); [Exec summary](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/comdemandcharge-execsummary.pdf)
- **Demand charges are a large share of the commercial bill.** NREL says they can be 30–70% of a commercial bill and that many customers don't fully understand how demand is measured and billed. — [NREL 68963 (2017)](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy17osti/68963.pdf). A secondary guide gives the same 30–70% range for small businesses. — [ElectricRates.org](https://electricrates.org/blog/small-business-electricity-rates-guide/)
- **Battery priorities change over time [vendor].** Installer guidance describes the EMS ranking outage reserve, demand-peak shaving, TOU arbitrage, and solar self-charging, with the ranking changing by season and tariff. — [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/solar-and-battery-storage-key-sizing-and-dispatch-factors-for-businesses/)
- **Priorities differ by facility type [vendor, illustrative, not survey].** Examples given: grocery stores focus on refrigeration backup plus demand-charge management, schools on resilience, self-consumption, and predictable savings. — [NeoVolta blog](https://neovolta.com/blog/what-commercial-and-industrial-energy-storage-projects-can-teach-the-industry)
- **Savings verified against guarantees [vendor].** Stem sells "Performance Reporting" and "Performance Guarantees" alongside utility-bill optimization, which implies that tracking verified savings is a purchase driver. Case-study figures, such as Granite Construction's $860K/yr across 5 sites, are unverified marketing. — [Stem Utility Bill Optimization overview (2021)](https://www.stem.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Stem-Solutions-Overview_Utility-Bill-Optimization_May-2021_Final.pdf); [Stem Granite case study (2023)](https://www.stem.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Stem-Case-Study_Granite-Construction_Jan-2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- The decision-driving metric for commercial storage is **dollars of demand charge avoided per billing period**, with the peak kW and its timestamp. This follows from the 42% vs. 8% gap and the 30–70% bill share. A homeowner-style "kWh produced" headline undersells the value of a commercial system.
- Because the general business surveys rank cost above carbon, ESG and carbon metrics are probably secondary for most SMBs. They matter mainly to firms with customer, investor, or regulatory reporting obligations. This is an inference; I found no SMB-specific survey on carbon-reporting needs.
- Verified savings against a baseline or guarantee is likely a core need for owners who financed systems on projected ROI. This is supported by the vendor practice of reporting against guarantees, not by an owner survey.

### Gaps
- I found no US survey, with a sample size, of commercial solar+storage owners' dashboard or metric priorities, whether from LBNL, NREL, Wood Mackenzie, SEIA, or a trade publication. A follow-up could check Wood Mackenzie/SEIA C&I reports or Canary Media and Utility Dive coverage of owner surveys.
- The NFIB sample size and the full question set were not retrieved (403 error).
- The ABB survey date is unconfirmed (about 2021–2022) and should be treated as older evidence.

## How do commercial dashboards present these metrics?

### Takeaway
Commercial platforms split into two groups. Asset and O&M views (SolarEdge ONE for C&I, Enphase Enlighten Manager, Tesla Powerhub) emphasize yield, performance ratio, availability, and alerts. Bill-optimization views (Stem Athena) break savings down by tariff line item, for example facility demand versus TOU demand. Fleet EV-charging software adds cost per mile, utilization, and peak-kW caps. First-party documentation for Generac PWRfleet, Span commercial, Wattbuild, and Schneider was not found within budget.

### Cited Findings
- **Stem Athena Explorer [vendor, 2021].** Shows the current month's utility-bill savings from optimized battery operation and breaks savings down by bill line item, such as facility and TOU demand charges. — [Athena Explorer one-pager (Nov 2021)](https://www.stem.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Stem-Athena-Explorer_One-Pager_-Nov2021_v3.pdf)
- **Stem Athena Portal [vendor, 2021].** A web interface for DER analysis, management, monitoring, and reporting. On PG&E Option S, Athena tracks both monthly and daily demand peaks and dispatches against predicted peaks. — [Athena overview (May 2021)](https://www.stem.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Stem-Solutions-Overview_Athena_May-2021_v10.pdf); [Stem Option S page](https://www.stem.com/one-pager-option-s)
- **Stem PowerTrack (third party, low confidence).** After the AlsoEnergy merger, products were unified under PowerTrack, which offers configurable reporting and event logs. The source is a comparison site with no reviews. — [rfp.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/specialty-industries/energy-utilities-software/battery-storage-software/stem/greenpower-monitor)
- **SolarEdge ONE for C&I [vendor].** A site dashboard with live KPIs (yield, performance ratio, site availability/uptime), comparative heatmaps and charts, and an alert system for proactive maintenance. — [SE ONE for C&I overview](https://knowledge-center.solaredge.com/sites/kc/files/se-one-for-c%26i-overview.pdf)
- **SolarEdge performance ratio [vendor].** PR is shown only when an on-site irradiance sensor or a paid satellite-PR service is configured. The PR chart can take up to one business day to appear. — [SolarEdge PR calculation note](https://knowledge-center.solaredge.com/sites/kc/files/monitoring_performance_ratio_calculation.pdf); [Site and account reports](https://knowledge-center.solaredge.com/sites/kc/files/monitoring-platform-site-and-account-reports.pdf)
- **Enphase Enlighten Manager [vendor].** Installer- and fleet-oriented: prioritizing maintenance across installations, per-panel power and AC/DC graphing, and one-button remote standby. I found no demand-charge features in the sources. — [Enphase FAQ on Enlighten Manager vs. free tier](https://www4.enphase.com/en-in/support/faq/what-difference-between-paid-enlighten-manager-and-free-panel-energy-data-offering); [PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/enphase_offers_simpler_microinverter_installation_and_operational_software)
- **Tesla Powerhub [vendor].** A monitoring and control platform for DERs, renewable plants, and microgrids. Tesla says it is used across more than 1 GWh of operating commercial sites, gives operational insight across aggregations of systems, and provides real-time performance metrics and SCADA-style elements. I found no first-party source confirming demand-charge reporting for SMB sites. — [Tesla Powerhub support page](https://www.tesla.com/support/powerhub)
- **Generac PWRfleet.** The only source found is a Sol Systems help page on linking PWRfleet sites for automatic SREC production reporting. This suggests PWRfleet exports production data for incentive and REC reporting, but its dashboard features are undocumented. — [Sol Systems help](https://help.srecs.solsystems.com/How-to-set-up-Generac-Power-Fleet-PWRfleet-for-automatic-SREC-reporting)
- **Fleet EV-charging dashboards [vendor].** Platforms report energy cost per mile, charger utilization, and avoided emissions, the last of which feeds ESG reports. — [Codibly](https://codibly.com/blog/articles/ev-fleet-charging-software). Some add per-vehicle kWh/mile and cost per vehicle. — [Fleetrabbit](https://fleetrabbit.com/article/ev-fleet-charging-management). Cost per mile is computed as kWh/mile × $/kWh at the hour charged. Demand is the highest 15-minute draw, typically $10–$25/kW. Staggering a 350 kW spike down to a flat 200 kW is cited as cutting the demand charge 30–50%. — [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/fleet-management/ev-fleet-charging-cost-tou-demand). The core job is "charge to departure, not to full," with per-time-of-day kW caps. — [Codibly](https://codibly.com/blog/articles/ev-fleet-charging-software); [Ampcontrol](https://ampcontrol.io/product-details/peak-shaving). Managed charging costs about $19–$51 per port per month. — [Fleetrabbit](https://fleetrabbit.com/article/ev-fleet-charging-management)
- **Depot vs. public charging cost (vendor, figures conflict).** One source gives about $0.10–$0.20/kWh all-in at the depot versus $0.35–$0.60/kWh on public networks. Another gives $0.20–$0.35/kWh for depot DCFC including demand charges. — [Heavy Vehicle Inspection](https://heavyvehicleinspection.com/industries/government/depot-charging-vs-public-charging-ev-fleets); [Heavy Vehicle Inspection cost guide](https://heavyvehicleinspection.com/blog/post/fleet-ev-charging-infrastructure-cost-guide)

### Inferences
- The recurring commercial "unit of value" is **$ saved per bill line item**, not kWh. Stem's line-item breakdown is the clearest pattern to copy for SMB savings attribution.
- Uptime and availability plus alerts are standard in asset dashboards (SolarEdge, Enphase). PR requires irradiance data, so production-vs-expected is often unavailable or estimated for SMB sites.
- Multi-site support appears in vendor positioning (Enphase "across installations," Tesla "aggregations," Stem multi-site case studies), but how these products present a portfolio rollup to an SMB owner is not documented in the sources I found.

### Gaps
- No first-party feature documentation was retrieved for Generac PWRfleet, Span commercial, Wattbuild, Schneider (EcoStruxure/Resource Advisor), Enphase IQ Commercial, or current (2025–2026) Stem PowerTrack screens. Several Stem sources date from 2021.
- I found no source on how these products present solar production vs. forecast to owners (as opposed to PR for O&M).

## How is carbon reported for businesses, and what do owners need for reporting vs. marketing?

### Takeaway
Corporate GHG inventories report Scope 2 under the GHG Protocol's dual method: location-based (grid-average factors) and market-based (contractual instruments such as RECs). As of late 2026 the 2015 Scope 2 Guidance is being revised: key text was expected mid-2026 and the full standard by end of 2027. The draft adds a more granular (hourly/local) location-based hierarchy and hourly/geographic matching for market-based clean-energy claims, with exemptions for smaller organizations. **Avoided emissions** and equivalents (trees, cars) are *not* inventory figures. EPA says AVERT-style avoided-emissions estimates are not for corporate GHG accounting. So "reporting" numbers (Scope 2 kgCO2e from grid import) and "marketing" numbers (avoided CO2, equivalencies) should be kept visibly separate.

### Cited Findings
- **Dual reporting retained.** The proposed revisions keep both location-based and market-based methods. — [Covington, Inside Energy & Environment (Nov 2025)](https://www.insideenergyandenvironment.com/2025/11/the-greenhouse-gas-protocols-proposed-scope-2-guidance-key-changes-and-considerations-for-stakeholders/); [PwC Viewpoint](https://viewpoint.pwc.com/us/en/pwc/in-briefs/ib_int202521.html)
- **ISB approval and timeline (Aug 1, 2025).** The Independent Standards Board voted 10–1 on July 14, 2025 to send the location- and market-based revisions to a 60-day public consultation (opened Oct 2025 per EY). Finalized key Scope 2 text was expected mid-2026, followed by a second consultation. The full revised Corporate Standard suite is targeted for end of 2027, with requirements phased in and a legacy clause for long-term contracts. — [GHG Protocol blog](https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/scope-2-standard-advances-isb-approves-consultation-market-and-location-based-revisions); [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/insights/ifrs/what-the-ghg-protocol-scope-2-consultation-means)
- **Location-based hierarchy.** The draft ranks factors by spatial boundary (local, operational grid, grid-wide, national), temporal granularity (hourly, monthly, annual), and type (consumption- or production-based). The more granular level is required only when activity data exists and the factors are free and public. — [Covington](https://www.insideenergyandenvironment.com/2025/11/the-greenhouse-gas-protocols-proposed-scope-2-guidance-key-changes-and-considerations-for-stakeholders/); [GHG Protocol blog](https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/scope-2-standard-advances-isb-approves-consultation-market-and-location-based-revisions)
- **Market-based hourly matching.** Hourly and geographic matching would be required for voluntary clean-energy claims. Smaller organizations get exemptions; one commentary cites a threshold of under 5 GWh aggregate consumption per market boundary. Profile-based or estimated hourly data is allowed. — [GHG Protocol blog](https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/scope-2-standard-advances-isb-approves-consultation-market-and-location-based-revisions); [Covington](https://www.insideenergyandenvironment.com/2025/11/the-greenhouse-gas-protocols-proposed-scope-2-guidance-key-changes-and-considerations-for-stakeholders/). EY argued hourly matching should stay optional until data infrastructure matures. — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/insights/ifrs/what-the-ghg-protocol-scope-2-consultation-means)
- **Avoided emissions moved out of Scope 2.** The "Marginal Impact Method," which would have netted load and procurement impacts into one metric, failed 7–4 and was moved to a cross-sectoral Actions and Market Instruments workstream. The Scope 2 consultation includes questions on avoided emissions and additionality. — [GHG Protocol blog](https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/scope-2-standard-advances-isb-approves-consultation-market-and-location-based-revisions)
- **EPA equivalencies use different factors for avoided vs. consumed electricity.** Since 2024 the Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies Calculator uses eGRID marginal and baseload factors for avoided electricity (efficiency or renewables) and annual average rates for electricity used. Older versions used an AVERT national marginal rate of about 7.07×10⁻⁴ tCO2/kWh, which is outdated. — [EPA calculations and references](https://www.epa.gov/energy/ghg-equivalencies-calculator-calculations-and-references); [Older EPA snapshot](https://19january2021snapshot.epa.gov/energy/greenhouse-gases-equivalencies-calculator-calculations-and-references_.html)
- **AVERT is not for accounting.** EPA says AVERT is for marginal emissions assessment, not emissions accounting, and warns renewable energy users against using avoided-emissions estimates to claim credit in corporate GHG reporting. The AVERT rates were updated in April 2024 to cover 2007–2023, including distributed PV. — [EPA AVERT page](https://www.epa.gov/node/255385)
- **SREC and production reporting.** Production data from monitoring platforms is exported automatically for SREC reporting (example: PWRfleet to Sol Systems), so production kWh doubles as an incentive and REC compliance input. — [Sol Systems help](https://help.srecs.solsystems.com/How-to-set-up-Generac-Power-Fleet-PWRfleet-for-automatic-SREC-reporting)
- **EV fleet avoided emissions** are surfaced in charging dashboards for ESG use [vendor]. — [Codibly](https://codibly.com/blog/articles/ev-fleet-charging-software)

### Inferences
- **What an SMB needs for reporting:** grid import kWh by month (hourly if available) × a location-based factor (eGRID subregion, CAISO hourly when the revised hierarchy applies) = location-based Scope 2. Market-based Scope 2 needs the utility's supplier-specific factor or REC/CCA product data. On-site solar consumed behind the meter lowers Scope 2 by reducing import. If the business sells the RECs, for example as SRECs, it should not also claim that solar as zero-carbon in market-based accounting. That last point is a standard GHG Protocol principle, inferred here; I did not fetch the 2015 Guidance text to cite it directly.
- **What an SMB uses for marketing:** "CO2 avoided," trees, and cars are communication metrics. They should use EPA's marginal factors and carry a label such as "estimated avoided emissions, not a GHG inventory figure."
- Most SMBs fall below the proposed hourly-matching thresholds (about 5 GWh), so monthly or annual location-based figures probably suffice for their reporting in the near term.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm whether the mid-2026 "finalized key text" was actually published, or what changed after the consultation. The current GHG Protocol site should be checked.
- I found no evidence on what share of US SMBs actually report Scope 2, for example under supply-chain requests or California SB 253, which applies to companies with more than $1B revenue and so is mostly out of SMB scope. This was not researched.
- ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager's treatment of on-site solar and green power was not retrieved.

## What confuses business owners?

### Takeaway
The best direct evidence, though older, shows that small business customers often do not even recognize a demand charge on their bill. The kW (rate) vs. kWh (quantity) distinction, how a single 15-minute interval sets the month's charge, and tariff-specific peak windows are the core confusions. In California, the Net Billing Tariff (NBT) values exports at Avoided Cost Calculator (ACC) rates rather than retail, which breaks the "1 kWh exported = 1 kWh credited" mental model inherited from NEM.

### Cited Findings
- **Bill comprehension study (OSTI 925403, likely LBNL, about 2007–2008, older evidence; date and authors unconfirmed because the fetch failed).** Very few small business people recognized a demand charge on their bill. At the smallest end, demand charges were almost entirely misunderstood. The authors concluded that lack of understanding limits how well TOU and demand charges reduce peak demand. — [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/925403-lkup3R)
- **"Charge Without a Cause?" (Acadia Center/Chernick et al., 2016, policy advocacy, not a survey).** Demand charges are complex and hard for small consumers to understand. Most utilities don't apply them to small commercial customers under roughly 20–50 kW. — [Acadia Center PDF](http://acadiacenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/Charge-Without-a-Cause-Final-7-18-16.pdf); [NARUC](https://pubs.naruc.org/pub.cfm?id=40288427-ADA1-0793-DB59-73A479E7F3FB)
- **Many commercial customers don't understand how demand is measured or billed (NREL, 2017).** — [NREL 68963](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy17osti/68963.pdf)
- **Mechanics owners must grasp (secondary guides).** Demand is the highest kW in any single 15-minute interval in the billing month × a $/kW rate, separate from kWh energy charges. Thresholds for being on a demand rate vary by utility, cited as about 10, 15, or 25 kW. — [ElectricRates.org](https://electricrates.org/blog/small-business-electricity-rates-guide/); [ElectricChoice](https://www.electricchoice.com/business-electricity/small-business/); [ComparePower (TX)](https://comparepower.com/demand-charges-explained/)
- **Tariff design changes solar's demand value.** Non-coincident demand charges give solar little value. Daytime peak-period demand charges give more. — [LBNL 2017](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/bibcite/reference/29690). PG&E Option S adds *daily* demand peaks on top of monthly ones [vendor description]. — [Stem Option S](https://www.stem.com/one-pager-option-s)
- **California NBT.** CPUC D.22-12-056 (Dec 15, 2022) replaced retail-rate export compensation with ACC values for interconnection applications on or after April 15, 2023. — [CPUC decision PDF](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Efile/G000/M499/K346/499346845.PDF); [SEIA commercial NBT factsheet (2023)](https://seia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Net-Billing-Commercial-CA-2023.pdf)
- **Commercial NBT specifics (sources partly conflict).** A 2022 proposed-decision summary says commercial ACC export values lock in for 5 years and float afterward. Confirm this against the final decision. — [Energy Toolbase](https://www.energytoolbase.com/blog/policyupdate/net-billing-tariff-revised-proposed-decision/). SEIA says legacy commercial NEM customers can stay on their tariff for 20 years from interconnection. — [SEIA factsheet](https://seia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Net-Billing-Commercial-CA-2023.pdf)
- **Virtual NBT and aggregation.** Commercial customers on VNBT, and all customers on NBTA, get no netting at benefitting meters: all allocated generation is valued at the export rate, which is the same for residential and commercial. — [CALSSA](https://calssa.org/vnem-and-nema)
- **California community solar.** An April 2026 CPUC proposed decision rejected the industry-backed Net Value Billing Tariff (NVBT). — [PV Magazine USA (Apr 9, 2026)](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/04/09/california-proposed-decision-on-community-solar-virtually-ensures-no-projects-will-be-built/)
- **Know-how gap.** 49% of businesses said they lack the know-how to act on energy challenges (ABB, older). — [ABB](https://new.abb.com/news/detail/100874/research-reveals-energy-concerns-impacting-business-competitiveness-the-workforce-and-decarbonization)

### Inferences
- Dashboards should show kW and kWh as visibly different quantities: a "peak" gauge or line versus an "energy" bar. They should also show *when* the month's peak was set and what it cost ($/kW × kW), because owners otherwise can't connect a 15-minute event to a large line item.
- Under NBT, export kWh should be shown alongside its *$ value* (ACC hourly rate), not just kWh. Self-consumed solar is worth retail and exported solar is worth much less, which is why storage that shifts exports into the evening matters. This mirrors the residential NEM 3.0 story.
- A billing-cycle-to-date "peak so far" with the remaining risk is likely the most actionable demand-charge view, because one bad interval late in the month sets the charge.

### Gaps
- I found no recent (2020s) survey with a sample size on SMB comprehension of demand charges or NBT. The main empirical study is from about 2007–2008.
- The actual ACC export price levels for commercial customers in 2026 and the final commercial lock-in terms were not verified from CPUC or utility tariff sheets.
- I found no evidence on owner confusion about battery dispatch logic, such as why the battery didn't discharge.

## Which metrics overlap with homeowner needs, so one component could serve both?

### Takeaway
There is substantial overlap in energy-flow and production metrics: solar kWh, consumption, grid import/export, battery state of charge and charge/discharge, self-consumption, export credit value, outage/backup status, alerts, and avoided-CO2 equivalencies. Commercial needs add demand ($/kW, peak timestamp, billing-cycle peak), bill line-item savings attribution, Scope 2 in reporting-grade form, multi-site rollups, uptime/PR for O&M, and EV fleet cost per mile. A shared component with optional "demand" and "portfolio" layers is supported by the evidence. The evidence is inferential, since no source directly compares residential and commercial dashboard needs.

### Cited Findings
- **Shared energy-flow model.** Generac's residential PWRview shows solar, storage, utility, and generator sources and where power goes, the same flow concept that commercial platforms present. — [New England Clean Energy](https://newenglandcleanenergy.com/?p=8267)
- **Shared TOU and export logic.** Batteries store excess solar for use when rates are highest. SEIA presents this in its commercial NBT factsheet, and the same logic drives residential NEM 3.0 storage. — [SEIA factsheet](https://seia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Net-Billing-Commercial-CA-2023.pdf)
- **Same export rate for both segments (California VNBT/NBTA).** Export rates are the same for residential and commercial accounts. — [CALSSA](https://calssa.org/vnem-and-nema)
- **Battery operating priorities are shared, with one extra for commercial.** Reserve for backup, TOU discharge, and solar charging apply to both. Demand-peak shaving is the commercial addition. — [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/solar-and-battery-storage-key-sizing-and-dispatch-factors-for-businesses/)
- **Commercial-only: demand charges.** Most utilities don't apply demand charges to small customers under about 20–50 kW, so residential and the smallest commercial accounts usually lack them. — [Acadia Center](http://acadiacenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/Charge-Without-a-Cause-Final-7-18-16.pdf)
- **Commercial-only: line-item savings and guarantees.** — [Stem Athena Explorer](https://www.stem.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Stem-Athena-Explorer_One-Pager_-Nov2021_v3.pdf)
- **Commercial-mostly: yield, PR, availability, comparative heatmaps.** — [SolarEdge ONE for C&I](https://knowledge-center.solaredge.com/sites/kc/files/se-one-for-c%26i-overview.pdf)
- **Commercial-only: fleet cost per mile, utilization, per-vehicle efficiency.** — [Codibly](https://codibly.com/blog/articles/ev-fleet-charging-software); [Fleetrabbit](https://fleetrabbit.com/article/ev-fleet-charging-management)

### Inferences
- **Reusable core (both segments):** live power flow (solar, battery, grid, loads, EV); daily, monthly, and annual solar kWh; consumption; import/export kWh plus export $ value; battery SOC and mode; outage/backup status; fault alerts; a savings-to-date and payback tracker; and avoided CO2 with equivalencies, clearly labeled as estimates.
- **Commercial extension layers:** (1) a demand layer with monthly and billing-cycle peak kW, peak timestamp, $/kW, kW avoided by the battery, and daily peaks for tariffs like Option S; (2) bill attribution separating energy (TOU $/kWh) from demand ($/kW) savings; (3) a reporting layer with Scope 2 location- and market-based figures from import kWh, exportable monthly, kept separate from avoided-emissions marketing numbers; (4) a portfolio layer with site rollup, site comparison, and uptime; (5) an EV fleet layer with cost per mile and charger kW caps.
- For a SMB on a non-demand tariff, such as a small storefront under the demand threshold, the residential component may serve almost unchanged.
