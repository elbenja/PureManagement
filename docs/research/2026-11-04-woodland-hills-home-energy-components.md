# Home energy components worth building first

**Research date:** November 4, 2026  
**Audience:** Owner of the modeled four-person detached home in Woodland Hills, Los Angeles, with rooftop solar, a home battery, an EV, and LADWP time-of-use service.  
**Purpose:** Rank reusable visual components before designing them. This is product research, not a visual specification or a claim that the simulated home represents a real customer's bill.

## Executive read

The useful homeowner questions are: **What is powering my home now? What is this costing or saving? Will the battery be available when I need it? What caused a change? Is the system behaving normally?** The live flow is an excellent entry point, but history, cost context, and plain-language explanations make it actionable. Tesla exposes flow by source and destination, time-scrubbable graphs, battery charge, and history at several scales; its newer Home Status feature explicitly explains *why* the system acted. [Tesla Energy Data](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/powerwall/mobile-app/energy-data), [Tesla Home Status](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/powerwall/mobile-app/home-status).

The best available California survey is directional: Enact surveyed 482 solar-owning homeowners in 2022, with over half from the Los Angeles region. In that sample, 36% used monitoring daily and 31% weekly; respondents preferred information about saving money over product recommendations. It is vendor research and cannot establish a Woodland Hills population ranking. [Enact survey and methodology](https://enact.solar/understanding-residential-solar-consumers-in-california/). A 2026 choice experiment in South Korea found rate alerts and appliance-level use more valued than carbon information, but its geography and tariff system differ. [Zhao and Huh, *Energy Policy*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2026.115303).

For this concept, prioritize **flow + context, cost/credit clarity, battery readiness, and usage drivers**. Keep CO2e as a supporting impact card. Treat actual bill balance, live outage status, fault detection, and verified appliance measurements as future capabilities: the current repository contains a deterministic simulation, not meter or device telemetry.

## Concepts to demystify in the components

1. **kW versus kWh:** “Power right now” versus “energy over this period.” Never add or compare them as if they are the same quantity. The repository's five-minute ledger supports both.
2. **Produced versus used:** Solar production can go to the home, EV, battery, or grid. Production alone does not say how much was self-used or saved. Tesla's [Energy Flow](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/powerwall/mobile-app/energy-data) makes destinations inspectable.
3. **Grid import versus export:** Show both directions explicitly. A single signed number or net total can hide important behavior.
4. **Export credit versus payment:** LADWP says eligible excess energy creates a credit applied to the associated bill and excludes certain charges; it is not an automatic cash payout. [LADWP NEM guidelines](https://www.ladwp.com/sites/default/files/2023-09/NEM%20Guidelines%20%28with%20April%202021%20technical%20modification%29%20%281%29.pdf).
5. **Modeled savings versus actual bill:** The simulation compares modeled energy costs with an all-grid counterfactual. It does not know the customer's billing adjustments, taxes, minimum charges, or opening NEM bank balance. Show the comparison formula and label it “modeled.”
6. **Battery percent versus backup time:** Backup duration depends on current and future load, reserve, solar, and what circuits are backed up. Tesla's [API description](https://developer.tesla.com/docs/fleet-api/endpoints/energy) bases a runtime estimate on remaining energy and recent load. This repository has no validated runtime selector.
7. **Low solar versus fault:** Clouds, daylight, and season can lower production. A fault claim needs measured hardware data, a baseline, and freshness/status evidence.
8. **Live versus replay:** This prototype advances one simulated day per real minute. Always pair “live” styling with a clear **Simulated 2026** indicator and local timestamp.

## Recommended first design set

Design **three reusable components** first: (1) a compact/expanded *Now & Flow* view, (2) a *Cost & Credit* card with rate-period context, and (3) a *Battery Readiness* card. Compose them in a small gallery at more than one width before building a dashboard page. Then add Usage Drivers and History. This sequence gives the homeowner an answer to “what, why, and what it means” while testing whether the pieces travel well between layouts.

The next design review should test plain-language comprehension with a few homeowner tasks: identify the current power source; distinguish earned credit from cash; explain why a hot weekday afternoon costs more; and tell whether the battery is charging or protecting reserve. A visual that looks attractive but fails those tasks should be revised.

## Source and confidence notes

- **Strong for rules and product behavior:** LADWP and Tesla first-party documentation. LADWP tariffs and NEM terms must be checked again before any real billing use.
- **Directional for preference:** Enact's 2022 online survey (482 qualified solar owners, vendor-run, LA-heavy); a 2026 South Korean choice experiment; small household field and lab studies. Their populations and setups differ from the modeled Woodland Hills home.
- **Low confidence but locally revealing:** Individual Reddit posts about LADWP NEM bank math and Tesla graph interpretation. They demonstrate possible confusion, not frequency.
- **Visual patterns only:** Mobbin screenshots show what existing apps put on screen. They do not prove that users prefer those designs. Search coverage for Tesla solar flow and Enphase was weak in this pass.
- **Repository fit:** `main` has the August simulator. The unmerged `codex/annual-energy-flow` branch adds 2026 weather, annual history, and outlook. All recommendations above that mention annual features refer to that branch. The project currently has no real device or utility connection.
