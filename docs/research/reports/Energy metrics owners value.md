# Lead with money, sources, and readiness

Owners of solar, batteries and EVs say they care most about **money** (bill savings and cost), and close behind that about **self-supply**: how much of their home ran on their own solar and battery. **Backup readiness** comes third. It is important to a sizeable minority but is not the everyday headline. Carbon motivates many owners, but they rarely ask for it as a metric, and the usual equivalents such as trees confuse people. Comprehension research shows people misjudge energy quantities by roughly **2.8x**. No study tests kW against kWh labeling directly, so the safe approach is to let time words carry the meaning ("now" for kW, "today/this month" for kWh) and to pair money with kWh. The Mobbin references that read best share one formula: a single large number with a small grey unit, one fixed color for each source, labels placed on the data, explicit time scope and freshness, and an "Estimated" qualifier on money. Tesla's Charge Stats, Petrol Savings and TOU-bar screens are the strongest examples, joined by Octopus, Rivian, YNAB, Monarch and Uber. For the simulated Woodland Hills home, the prioritized top six are: (1) live energy flow with a one-line "now" explanation, (2) modeled savings against an all-grid bill, (3) where the home's energy came from (self-powered share), (4) battery readiness, (5) grid exchange (import cost against export *bill credit*, with time-of-use context), and (6) period history compared with the home's own past. Carbon, appliance breakdowns, curtailment and system health, and business-only layers (demand charges, Scope 2) are deliberately ranked lower. Two caveats limit Tesla-specific conclusions. Mobbin's Tesla account has **no Powerwall or solar site**, and **tesla.com support pages returned HTTP 403** during this research pass.

## Bills come first, self-supply a close second, backup third

No representative US study ranks dashboard metrics for solar, battery and EV owners, so this ranking is built from motivations and adjacent evidence. The best US data comes from a vendor panel. **Enact surveyed 482 qualified California and New York solar owners in 2022**, more than half of them in the Los Angeles region. Saving money was the top reason for going solar (**36%**), with environment and "supplying my own power" tied at **25%**, and respondents wanted more information on how to save money ([Enact](https://enact.solar/understanding-residential-solar-consumers-in-california/)). Battery buyers show the same order with a tighter spread: **30% cite rate savings, 29% solar self-supply and 26% backup**. Meanwhile **67% of installers** believed backup was the main motivation ([ESS News on EnergySage H1 2025](https://www.ess-news.com/2025/12/29/the-installer-customer-mismatch-on-residential-battery-motivations/)). A self-selected Australian survey (n=597, 2026) found **90.1%** put lower bills in their top three, **82.6%** using more of their own solar, **85.7%** fighting climate change, and only **40.9%** avoiding blackouts ([Rewiring Australia](https://rewiringaustralia.org/impact/research/home-battery-survey-report)). Together these support treating self-supply as its own value, not just a proxy for savings, and treating backup as a "check when needed" metric rather than the headline.

How people actually use the apps points the same way. In the Enact panel, **36% checked daily and 31% weekly**, and LA respondents were **twice as likely** as other regions to check daily. However, **13% never used the app** ([Enact](https://enact.solar/understanding-residential-solar-consumers-in-california/)). Engagement fades even in motivated samples. In a German field experiment with 111 PV households, feedback alone raised self-consumption about **3–4%**, while a default setting raised it about **16%** ([TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:640a2c1f-2545-49ad-994e-b3594ac71180)). Owners mostly open the app for reassurance and to explain surprises, such as Tesla forum threads about "wonky Powerwall numbers" or charge lost during an outage ([TMC](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/wonky-powerwall-numbers-is-this-normal.223284/latest)). That implies two modes. One is a daily glance answering "is everything OK, and what is happening?" The other is an occasional deep dive into bills and anomalies.

What people say and what changes their behavior can differ. In an RCT in Los Angeles family housing, messages framed around environment and health cut use by **8.2%** (up to 19% for families with children). The cost frame produced no significant savings, and among high users it even increased use ([PNAS/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4330782)). That study is small (118 apartments) and its participants were renters, not solar owners. It still argues for keeping a carbon card rather than dropping it. Money stays the headline because a solar home's savings are already locked in by the hardware: the money frame reassures more than it motivates.

Business owners rank cost even more clearly above carbon. In an ABB survey of about 2,300 business leaders, **61%** named energy cost reduction as their top priority and **40%** included carbon among priorities ([ABB](https://new.abb.com/news/detail/100874/research-reveals-energy-concerns-impacting-business-competitiveness-the-workforce-and-decarbonization)). Their unit of value differs from a homeowner's, though. Demand charges can make up **30–70% of a commercial bill** ([NREL](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy17osti/68963.pdf)). Modeled solar-plus-storage cuts median demand charges about **42%, against about 8% for solar alone** ([LBNL](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/publications/solar-storage-synergies-managing)). Stem's dashboards accordingly break savings down by bill line item ([Stem Athena Explorer](https://www.stem.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Stem-Athena-Explorer_One-Pager_-Nov2021_v3.pdf)). No survey of US commercial owners' metric priorities, with a stated sample size, was found. The overlap is still clear: flow, production, consumption, import/export with credit value, battery state, outage status and avoided CO2 serve both segments. Demand ($/kW, peak timestamp), line-item attribution, Scope 2 reporting and multi-site rollups are commercial add-ons. The simulated home is on a residential R-1B tariff, so the top six serve homeowners first, and small businesses on non-demand tariffs nearly unchanged.

## Comprehension fails on units, denominators and credits

The confusions cluster in five places, and each one shapes the copy rules below. **Units:** in general-public samples, about half of North American bill payers say they understand kW and kWh ([E Source](https://web-ivr.esource.com/printpdf/207833)), 18% of Australians did not know their bill's unit ([SolarQuotes](https://solarquotes.com.au/blog/energy-literacy-australia-mb3100)), and only 47.3% of Japanese respondents correctly picked kWh ([Selectra](https://selectra.jp/energy/survey/electricity-literacy-survey-2026)). People underestimate energy use by about **2.8x** ([White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/74518/)), and their estimates swing from **2.5x to 18.3x** depending on the reference object they are given ([PMC3044394](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3044394)). Judgments are more accurate in energy units than in money, and over monthly periods rather than hourly ones ([White Rose eTheses](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/18286)). No controlled study tests kW-versus-kWh labeling, so this is a real evidence gap. The defensible approach is to pair money with kWh, lead with period totals, and keep "now" values clearly labeled as live.

**Denominators:** "self-powered," "self-sufficiency" and "energy independence" mean the share of *home consumption* not served by the grid (Tesla, Home Assistant). "Self-consumption" means the share of *solar* used on site (SolarEdge). Apps rarely say which one they show ([Tesla Self-Powered](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/powerwall/mobile-app/self-powered); [Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/dashboards/energy/); [Schneider](https://blog.se.com/infrastructure-and-grid/power-management-metering-monitoring-power-quality/2019/07/01/optimizing-your-solar-investment-was-never-easier/)). A FoxESS user saw "0% self-sufficiency" on a sunny day because the formula clamps negative results to zero ([FoxESS forum](https://foxesscommunity.com/viewtopic.php?p=6404)). Tesla owners are puzzled when exporting at noon and importing at night lowers Self-Powered % ([TMC](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/solar-offset-vs-energy-offset.206118/)). Tesla uses "Self-Powered" both for the metric and for an operating mode, which adds to the confusion.

**Battery percent against usable energy and runtime:** owners mistrust the gap between % and the kWh they actually got, and a hidden 5% reserve makes Tesla's app % differ from its API % ([GitHub #82297](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/issues/82297)). Base Power shows a good disclosure pattern. Its backup estimate uses the last 15 minutes of load, is marked with a tilde, rises as use falls, and excludes solar ([Base Power help](https://help.basepowercompany.com/en/articles/10196033)). FranklinWH states that its Backup Remaining figure assumes no grid, generator or solar input ([FranklinWH manual](https://e-uploads.franklinwh.com/website/news/4f5a997697fa608b31aaa9b0151a3295.pdf)).

**Bills against apps:** owners are blindsided by true-ups and by fixed charges that solar cannot offset, often because their monitoring shows production but not consumption ([r/solar thread](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/solar/comments/1jsxgo4/bill_wrong/mmce9h8/?context=3)). Enphase's documented answer is to read the utility bill next to the app ([Enphase](https://enphase.com/learn/home-energy/explore-your-system/utility-bill-and-enphase-app)). LADWP applies eligible excess energy as a credit on the bill, not as a cash payout ([LADWP NEM guidelines](https://www.ladwp.com/sites/default/files/2023-09/NEM%20Guidelines%20%28with%20April%202021%20technical%20modification%29%20%281%29.pdf)).

**Carbon:** trees and CO2 metrics confused participants in a small eco-feedback study ([Aziz](https://cmu.academia.edu/AzizanAziz)). CO2 by weight beat "saving trees" in hypothetical choices ([arXiv 2407.08897](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08897)). An energy-native unit ("light-bulb minutes") shifted choices in one small experiment ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcli/v9y2019i1d10.1038_s41558-018-0354-z.html)).

Labeling values as modeled costs little trust. Across five experiments (n=5,780), stating uncertainty reduced trust only slightly, mostly when the uncertainty was expressed in vague words, and numeric ranges barely affected trust in the source ([van der Bles et al.](https://core.ac.uk/download/305110478.pdf)). This supports the project's rule that every value is labeled simulated or modeled, ideally with a method note one tap away rather than hedging language.

On visual form, no controlled study compares Sankey or flow diagrams, stacked bars and rings for household energy. A systematic review warns that poorly chosen visualizations reduce effectiveness ([NTU IRep](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/46139)). The conventions owners have already learned are a four-node live flow, Day/Week/Month/Year tabs, one headline % ratio, and battery % with reserve ([Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/dashboards/energy/); [Sense](https://sense.com/consumer-blog/understanding-the-sense-app/)). Colors are not standardized across apps (SolarEdge uses green for production, Sense uses green for total use), so every chart needs direct labels.

## Tesla's restraint and five other apps set the visual bar

The Mobbin screens that read best do so through restraint. Each uses about three type sizes, puts units small and grey beside a big numeral, gives each meaning one accent color, and places labels on the data instead of in legends. **Tesla Charge Stats** puts "Total Charged 391 kWh" and "Total Spent $115" side by side as equal-weight heroes over daily bars, with a location split shown as labeled icons rather than a pie ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/71b09873-9a5b-4a0a-83a8-b50e82c53264?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). **Tesla Petrol Savings** derives "$119 Saved" from a visible spent-against-equivalent comparison and labels it "Estimated Values (i)" ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/f332f73c-408f-46ec-9201-de21fdd6cfb2?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). The **Tesla TOU bar** fits a full day of Off-Peak, Mid-Peak and Peak into one traffic-light strip ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/115aa3aa-0efa-413f-869c-2fd19eda6d2f?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). Its pricing view sets buy and sell $/kWh side by side, which makes the asymmetry between import cost and export value obvious ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/f40214c4-e79e-4b55-9440-551fb622620c?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). The only Powerwall flow on Mobbin is a small "Demo Powerwall" teaser, which places "SOLAR 6.5 kW / MODEL 3 0.8 kW / HOME 0.8 kW" around a house ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/666b888d-e943-40ca-a1ac-72de35a934ba?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). It confirms Tesla's habit of using kW on the live render and kWh in history, a split worth copying exactly. Tesla also shows the main anti-pattern: its charging card packs "4 kW · +0 kWh · 19/32A · 242V" into one grey string ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/c0a37ffb-23c3-44d7-a3e8-fd50735582e0?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)).

Outside Tesla, **Octopus Energy** is the closest analog to a bill-aware dashboard. It combines Day/Week/Month/Year tabs, a date pager, an "ESTIMATED COST" hero with a (?) chip, and a kWh/£ toggle on the chart itself ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/f74e0586-934f-43cf-a048-392c868b5893?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). Its tariff screen shows the standing charge as a hero, a direct template for the $12 service charge ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/f95ffb41-c106-4db6-b0a5-012d235211f7?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). **Rivian** contributes a centered-total donut with an icon grid and a "Synced at 19:26" freshness label ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/4df8142b-b9da-44bf-8ad1-e3571ef960ed?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)), and a charging bar whose hatched gap runs up to a limit tick ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/fdd5e60c-a1d2-4525-94ce-8b4bffe986a8?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). **YNAB**'s single 100% stacked bar over a category list is the cleanest "where did it come from" pattern ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/f9387a42-18ab-4c36-a7f5-425845680ff8?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). **Monarch**'s labeled Sankey works for a desktop period view ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/92c2b32c-20a0-4487-9b6c-3f32cb464893?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). **Uber**'s "Estimated CO₂ saved" shows one everyday equivalent per illustrated row ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/f06bd89a-24cf-461d-a85b-6c94a32f0b11?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). These are design judgments made from screenshots, not usability results. Mobbin shows what apps put on screen, not what users understand.

## The top six metrics and the components to build for them

The ranking weighs four things: owner value (section 1), comprehension risk (section 2), whether the simulation can supply honest data, and Mobbin pattern strength. It is a design recommendation, not a measured popularity order. One shared design system underlies all six. It assigns one fixed color each to solar, battery, grid import, grid export, EV and home. It uses kW only in "now" contexts and kWh only with a named period. It shows a persistent "Simulated 2026" badge with a local timestamp, and an (i) on every derived number that opens its formula.

| Rank | Metric | Plain-language question | Evidence strength | Serves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Live energy flow + "now" sentence (kW) | What is powering my home right now? | Moderate (strong cross-app convention; no comprehension test of flows) | Both |
| 2 | Modeled savings vs all-grid, with cost (USD + kWh) | Is this system saving me money? | Strong for stated preference; mixed for behavior change | Both (business needs line items) |
| 3 | Self-powered share: home energy by source (kWh, %) | How much of my home ran on my own solar and battery? | Moderate (self-supply ~25–29% motivation; no study of the % metric itself) | Mostly homeowners |
| 4 | Battery readiness: SoC, kWh above reserve, ~backup hours, outage | Will the battery carry me if the grid goes down? | Moderate (backup cited by ~26% of buyers; runtime confusion documented anecdotally) | Both |
| 5 | Grid exchange: import cost vs export bill credit, TOU, credit bank | What did I pay the grid, what credit did I bank, and why was this hour expensive? | Moderate (bill confusion anecdotal but recurring; LADWP rules first-party) | Both |
| 6 | Period history vs own past (kWh by source, delta) | What changed this week or month, and why? | Moderate-to-strong (self-comparison supported; time tabs universal) | Both |

### 1. Live energy flow answers "what is powering my home now"

Every benchmarked app leads with a four-node live flow (Solar, Home, Battery, Grid), and the simulation adds EV as a fifth node ([Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/dashboards/energy/); [FranklinWH](https://www.franklinwh.com/support/articles/detail/what-features-does-the-franklinwh-app-provide)). Flows are the one encoding that shows *direction*, which bars cannot. The comprehension literature still advises keeping flows to few nodes and pairing them with conventional charts. Tesla's Home Status adds a short "why the system is doing this" summary ([Tesla Home Status](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/powerwall/mobile-app/home-status)), which is the model for a one-sentence explanation. **Component:** a spatial node diagram with kW callouts placed on each node, following the Tesla Demo Powerwall teaser and the [Tesla My Home](https://mobbin.com/screens/e9185aa7-9613-424c-b995-08f17e9e2ae3?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp) render. It carries animated lines for active transfers and a sentence underneath ("Solar is powering the home and charging the battery"). A compact variant uses Starlink's "Average | Current" twin hero ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/6bef1263-c4dd-45ce-b6c4-0fbc73d36ab1?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)), and a desktop period variant uses the Monarch Sankey in kWh. **Data:** live nodes (kW, direction, status, including grid `outage`), explicit source→destination transfers, timestamp, and conditions. Served-against-requested home demand and backupPowerLimited show when the battery's 5 kW limit is throttling the home during an outage. Everything needed exists. **Copy:** "Now · 2:35 PM · Simulated," with every value in kW. Never total kW into energy. During an outage, the grid node reads "Grid outage (simulated)," not a bare zero.

### 2. Modeled savings answer "is this saving me money"

Money is the top stated motivation for both segments, and real-time cost or bill displays are among the features in-home-display users value most ([Electronic Specifier on the ESMIG review](https://www.electronicspecifier.com/?p=123189)). Money works best paired with kWh, because people judge kWh more accurately and the money frame alone can license extra use in low-price settings. **Component:** Tesla Charge Stats twin heroes ("Grid energy 412 kWh · Modeled cost $118") above a Tesla Petrol Savings-style comparison: actual modeled cost against an all-grid bar, with the savings derived visibly from the two and an "Estimated (i)" label. Add an Octopus kWh/$ toggle and period tabs. **Data:** interval import cost, export credit, the all-grid counterfactual and savings, and history totals for 24h/7d/30d/month/YTD. Everything needed exists. **Copy:** "Modeled savings vs an all-grid bill." Avoid "you saved" or "earned." The (i) states the formula (all-grid cost − [import cost − export bill credit]) and notes that taxes, minimum charges and the real LADWP bank are not modeled. **Business owners** would need this split into energy ($/kWh) and demand ($/kW) savings, Stem-style. R-1B has no demand charge, so that remains a known missing layer.

### 3. Self-powered share answers "how much ran on my own energy"

Self-supply is a distinct value, ranking just behind money (25–29% of US motivations, 82.6% top-three in Australia). Tesla elevates Self-Powered % to its Impact tab ([Tesla Impact Cards](https://tesla.com/support/energy/powerwall/mobile-app/impact-cards)), and Home Assistant users lobbied for a self-sufficiency gauge before it existed ([HA community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/energy-dashboard-self-sufficiency-gauge-card/341692)). The metric is also the most confusable in the set, so the component should *show the composition* rather than a lone ring. **Component:** a YNAB-style 100% stacked bar labeled "Where your home's energy came from: Solar 58% · Battery 21% · Grid 21%," with kWh on each segment and a list beneath. On compact surfaces, use a WHOOP-style single ring ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/eb710c39-dc18-4cd5-954d-d71b96c42519?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)) or the Rivian centered-total donut. **Data:** derivable from the source→destination transfers aggregated over the period. There is **no precomputed self-powered field**, so the product must define one. The proposal is (solar→home + battery→home + solar→EV + battery→EV) ÷ total served home + EV demand, with an explicit decision on whether EV load counts and whether battery energy that came from the grid counts as "self." Never clamp to an unexplained 0%. **Copy:** "Self-powered (share of your home's electricity that came from your solar and battery, today)." Don't name it after the Tesla operating mode, and don't show "solar used at home %" next to it for lay users.

### 4. Battery readiness answers "will the battery carry me"

Backup ranks third, but it is the metric owners check after surprises, and its confusions (% against usable kWh, runtime volatility) are well documented. **Component:** a horizontal fill bar adapted from the Rivian charging progress view, with the 15% reserve drawn as a labeled tick *below* the fill, borrowing Garmin's dashed goal line ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/6bb49866-7232-4604-b0c6-c2a1fb49e058?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). It has a state label (Charging from solar / Discharging / Idle / Holding reserve) and a secondary line "~3.4 h at recent use (estimate)." The history drill-in uses a Garmin Body Battery-style SoC curve, simplified to avoid its legend-heavy overlay ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/920a4278-e04a-42eb-a7d1-b6e7f32be3e1?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). **Data:** socKwh, socPercent, the 15% reserve, battery node direction and kW, backupHoursEstimate (battery-only, last-60-minute average load), backupPowerLimited, and grid outage status. "kWh available above reserve" is a simple derivation: socKwh − 2.025 kWh. **Copy:** lead with kWh above reserve plus %, and always show the tilde and "estimate, battery only, based on the last hour's use; not a guarantee." When backupPowerLimited is true, say "Home demand exceeds the battery's 5 kW limit (simulated)." Mobbin has no example of a reserve line drawn *under* the current SoC, so this tick is new design work.

### 5. Grid exchange answers "what did I pay, what credit did I bank, and why"

This metric addresses the bill-against-app confusion head on and teaches why timing matters. **Component:** three parts. First, Ubank In & Out paired monthly bars, relabeled "Grid import cost" against "Export bill credit," with signed totals ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/beea14a3-2aea-452f-b31c-d108331d7add?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). Second, a Tesla-style 24-hour LADWP R-1B rate bar with a "now" marker, plus the buy/sell price view for the asymmetry. Third, an Octopus-style statement card showing modeled amount due, the $12 service charge as a fixed line, and the carried-forward credit bank. **Data:** interval import cost, export credit, import/export kWh from transfers, the rate period, and monthly modeled statements with credit bank and service charge. Everything needed exists. **Copy:** "Export bill credit (applied to your modeled bill)." Never use "sold," "earnings" or "income." The heading reads "Modeled statement, not an LADWP bill." Show kWh and $ side by side for each direction. Never show a single net-signed number alone, because it hides two-way behavior.

### 6. Period history answers "what changed, and compared with what"

Comparison with one's own past is the safest supported comparison format ([Froehlich et al., CHI 2010](https://makeabilitylab.cs.washington.edu/media/publications/Froehlich_TheDesignOfEcoFeedbackTechnology_CHI2010.pdf); [E3T NW](https://www.e3tnw.org/research/energy/downloads/smart-metering-report.pdf)). Normative "similar homes" reports have the strongest behavioral evidence overall, about **2% savings across ~600,000 households** ([MIT CEEPR](https://ceepr.mit.edu/workingpaper/social-norms-and-energy-conservation)), but a single simulated home has no peers to compare against. **Component:** Octopus tabs plus a date pager ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/8cf9882b-c968-4526-8452-f79834c10edf?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)), with a Tesla Vehicle Year-style hero ("↓ Imported 155.0 kWh") and three-gridline bars ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/1f99eb66-657e-478a-a519-36cd212b963f?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). It carries a CRED-style delta chip ("↓ 38 kWh vs last month") ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/7f33e880-f2cb-450b-a1bd-90def15a9e64?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)), a Revolut average line ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/69913e7a-7c90-4051-9fe1-84f2f55920d9?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)), and Fi-style greyed future intervals ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/7ceb0103-5a3e-456b-86fd-dc767d55e4d0?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)). Appliance-category kWh opens as a drill-in that explains "why use rose." **Data:** history totals and sampled series for 24h/7d/30d/month/YTD, plus appliance-category kWh. **Missing:** a prior-period comparison total and delta. The simulation starts in 2026, so same-month-last-year is unavailable. Pair "vs last month" with a season or temperature note rather than claiming weather normalization. **Copy:** "Used this month: 612 kWh (simulated)." Name the exact date span. Design the sparse early-year state, avoiding Tesla's single bar against 11 blanks with no note.

## Carbon, appliances and business layers deliberately rank lower

**Avoided CO2e** sits just outside the top six as a secondary impact card. Climate motivates owners, but carbon information was valued below rate alerts and appliance monitoring in a 2026 choice experiment ([Zhao and Huh](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2026.115303)), and trees confuse people. Use an Uber or LARQ-style card ([LARQ](https://mobbin.com/screens/4c825c69-f6e7-48cc-9f54-e40784443255?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)) with kg CO2e as the hero and at most one equivalent, such as miles driven or gallons of gasoline, never trees. The project's fixed **0.229 kg/kWh** factor is well below EPA's figures: the national "used" rate is 0.394 kg/kWh, and the California marginal "avoided" rate is about 1,055 lb/MWh (≈0.48 kg/kWh before line losses) ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/energy/ghg-equivalencies-calculator-calculations-and-references)). Label the result "Estimated avoided CO2e (simulation factor 0.229 kg/kWh)" so it is not mistaken for EPA or inventory accounting, which EPA explicitly separates from avoided-emissions estimates ([EPA AVERT](https://www.epa.gov/node/255385)). **Appliance breakdowns** are popular in stated-preference studies, but causal evidence for them is weak ([arXiv 1605.00962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.00962.pdf)), and the categories here are simulated, so they live inside history. **The EV charging window** folds into flow (rank 1) and TOU (rank 5) rather than getting its own card. Tesla's "TOU at Home" share bars ([Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/15908683-d7a2-4e5f-bc7d-f4675ecafeda?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=ai-assistant&utm_source_platform=mcp)) are a good later "load-shifting" view. **Actual against potential solar and curtailment, system health, and the 7-day outlook** are useful for showing the simulation and for business O&M (SolarEdge performance ratio and availability ([SolarEdge](https://knowledge-center.solaredge.com/sites/kc/files/se-one-for-c%26i-overview.pdf))), but the simulation cannot diagnose real faults. **Solar Offset %** suits sizing more than daily use. **Demand charges, Scope 2 reporting, multi-site rollups and fleet cost per mile** are business-only and not modeled by an R-1B home.

The main evidence gaps are these. No representative US owner survey ranks dashboard metrics. No study tests kW-versus-kWh labels, the "self-powered %" metric, flow diagrams against bars, or battery gauges. There is no 2020s survey of SMB comprehension of demand charges or metric priorities. Tesla Powerwall screens (live flow at full size, Backup Reserve, Storm Watch, Impact, Solar Value) are **absent from Mobbin**, whose Tesla capture covers only a Wall Connector and a Model 3. **tesla.com support pages returned 403** in this pass, so Tesla definitions rest on indexed snippets and should be checked in the app before quoting. Mobbin also has no net-metering or credit-bank explainer screen. The Enphase, SolarEdge, Sense and Span apps did not surface in generic Mobbin searches.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing from "which numbers to show" to "which honest *pairs* to show." Each top metric works best as a pair that answers its own follow-up question: kW now alongside the sentence explaining it, modeled cost alongside the all-grid counterfactual, self-powered % alongside the kWh composition behind it, battery % alongside kWh above reserve and an estimated runtime, import cost alongside export *credit*, and this period alongside the last one. Single bare numbers are what generate the forum confusion. The cheapest and most valuable next step is the one the earlier research proposed and no study has done. Run a short task test with a handful of owners on the six components: name the current source, tell a bill credit from cash, explain a peak-hour cost, read battery hours as an estimate, and find what changed this month. Iterate on whichever component fails.

The simulation is unusually well placed here. Because it records explicit source→destination transfers, it can show composition and causes that many real monitors without consumption metering cannot. Only two fields need adding before design starts: a defined self-powered share and a prior-period delta.
